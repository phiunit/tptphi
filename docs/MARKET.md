## 1. Platform shape — where the space is on TPT

- TPT reportedly has **7M+ users and 5M+ teacher-created resources**; **~52% of TPT users are
  K–5 teachers**, and the majority of the ~3M resources target K–5. **Printables are ~70% of
  available materials.** Source (third-party aggregator analysis, treat as directional):
  https://www.accio.com/business/best_selling_resources_teachers_pay_teachers
- Seller earnings are extremely top-heavy — most sellers make $0–$100/month; the top ~1% earn more
  than the bottom 99% combined. TPT pays **50% basic / 75% premium** (annual fee).
  Source: https://seolumina.com/blog/how-much-do-tpt-sellers-make-in-2026-real-data-income-breakdown
  *(third-party estimate; the 50/75 split is TPT's published structure)*
- TPT itself markets middle-school financial literacy as a category on its own consumer blog —
  a signal it's a promoted, high-traffic vertical:
  https://blog.teacherspayteachers.com/financial-literacy-middle-school/

**Read:** the crowding is in K–5 printables. Grades 6–8 "future skills" is a comparatively thin
slice of a very large marketplace, and print-first is the *dominant* format, not a handicap.

## 3. The genuine gaps (evidence-based)

### 3.1 GAP #1 — AI literacy you can teach with **no student AI access**. (Strongest gap found.)

Two facts collide:

1. **Demand exists and is now mainstream in our exact grade band.** In an EdWeek Research Center
   nationally representative survey of 499 teachers, principals and district leaders (fielded
   Dec–Jan), **73% said students in grades 6–8 in their district are receiving lessons on what AI is
   and how to use it responsibly** (vs. nearly 8 in 10 for high school).
   https://www.edweek.org/technology/are-ai-literacy-lessons-now-the-norm-what-new-survey-data-show/2026/03

2. **Student access to AI is being cut off.** On **Sept 2, 2026** — one day before this document —
   New York City, the nation's largest district, announced a **one-year ban on student-facing
   generative AI and companion chatbots for 2K through 8th grade** for the 2026–27 school year,
   affecting roughly **600,000 students**, and said it will disable AI components in **38+
   previously allowed programs**. The same policy sets recommended screen-time caps of **45
   minutes/day for grades 6–8** and 30 min/day for 3–5.
   https://edition.cnn.com/2026/09/02/tech/new-york-city-classroom-ai-ban ·
   https://www.engadget.com/2249790/nyc-generative-ai-ban-in-public-schools-through-eighth-grade/ ·
   https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2026/09/02/new-york-city-bans-ai-use-in-schools-through-eighth-grade
   EdWeek is already asking whether other districts follow:
   https://www.edweek.org/technology/new-york-restricts-ai-in-elementary-and-middle-schools-will-other-districts-follow/2026/09

Meanwhile the AI-literacy products we could identify lean on **live model access** (Pedagogy Cloud's
"compare responses from multiple generative AI models"; Scratch/Slides-based work elsewhere).

**The gap:** grades 6–8 AI literacy that is **100% unplugged, print-only, and standards-cited** —
teachable in a district that has banned student AI and capped screen time. Our CLAUDE.md already
mandates an unplugged variant in every tech lesson. **We should stop treating that as a courtesy
feature and make it the headline claim.** This is a now-or-never window: NYC's ban is one week old.

*Caveat:* the ban is one district's one-year moratorium. It is a strong leading indicator, not proof
of a national trend. Do not claim a trend in listing copy.

### 3.2 GAP #2 — Middle-school personal finance built to a **middle-school mandate**, not a HS course

Nearly all mandate pressure and nearly all TPT supply are aimed at **high school**. See §4. But:

- **New York** now requires personal finance instruction **by the end of grade 8**, beginning with
  the **2026–27 school year** (grades 5–12 in 26–27; K–4 in 27–28), per a Commissioner's Regulation
  §100.2 amendment approved by the Board of Regents in March 2026.
  https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/personal-finance-education ·
  https://www.nysed.gov/memo/standards-instruction/amendment-section-1002-regulations-commissioner-education-require ·
  https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/personal-finance-education-faq
- NY's five required topic strands map almost exactly onto our finance line: **budgeting & money
  management, credit & debt management, earning income, risk management, saving & investing** —
  and districts may deliver them **embedded in existing subjects**, which is precisely what a
  print, drop-in lesson serves.
  https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/personal-finance-topics-grade-bands
- **Oklahoma** embeds personal finance across **grades 7–12** (Passport to Financial Literacy, 2007).
  https://excelined.org/2025/03/04/financial-literacy-education-in-the-united-states-landscape-analysis-and-next-steps/
- Broadly, **K–8 financial education remains inconsistent and under-specified** across states —
  the analysis above describes middle-grade requirements as lacking clear structure. That is the
  supply gap restated: publishers chase the HS graduation requirement.

**Note for us specifically:** NY's "**risk management**" strand is a named requirement, and
`finance-risk-boss` ("Risk Like a Boss: Decision-Making & Expected Value") sits directly on it.
Very few competitor products in this band appear to address risk at all — the market is saturated
with *budgeting*.

### 3.3 GAP #3 — Middle-school career/entrepreneurship exposure is now **legally required in more places than the supply reflects**

- **Every U.S. state has some middle-school career-exploration policy or requirement** as of 2024.
  https://www.britebound.org/insights/unpacking-states-efforts-to-prioritize-and-facilitate-middle-school-career-exploration ·
  https://www.ecs.org/states-putting-early-career-exploration-to-work-for-students/
- **Ohio: beginning July 1, 2026**, all city, local and exempted village districts must provide
  **career-technical education exposure to students in grades 7 and 8**.
  https://ennisbritton.com/blog/2026/ctc-corner-ohios-middle-school-career-technical-education-mandate-legal-considerations-and-practical-implications ·
  https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Career-Tech/CTE-Middle-Grade-Programming/Middle-School-FAQ.pdf.aspx
- **Utah** requires a grades 7–8 College & Career Awareness course; **Pennsylvania** requires a
  grade-8 career plan; **Virginia** requires a middle-school career-investigation course; **Maine**
  requires districts to provide CTE access in grades 6–8. Same two sources as above, plus
  https://www.maine.gov/doe/learning/cte/schools/middleschool ·
  https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/newsroom/pennsylvania-leads-the-nation-in-middle-school-career-exploration-ranks-highest-in-national-study

Supply, meanwhile, is HS-CTE-shaped (§2.3) and digital-leaning. **A print, no-prep, 6–8
career-and-entrepreneurship kit sized for a 45-minute middle-school period is under-served** — and
Ohio just created a statewide buyer with a hard date.

### 3.4 GAP #4 — Everything aligned to CSTA **2017** is now dated

CSTA released the **2026 PK–12 Computer Science Standards** at its 2026 annual conference in New
Orleans — the **first major revision since 2017** — emphasizing creativity, ethics, data,
human-centered design, and explicitly integrating AI.
https://csteachers.org/2026-csta-pk-12-computer-science-standards/ ·
https://www.cstawa.org/introducing-the-2026-csta-pk-12-computer-science-standards/ ·
https://dl.acm.org/doi/book/10.1145/3820482

Our own `docs/STANDARDS.md` cites 2017-era codes (2-AP-10, 2-IC-20/21). **Every competitor product
in AI and game design is in the same position, and most will not update.** Being early to cite the
2026 standards is a differentiator and a keyword ("2026 CSTA standards aligned").

> **Do not act on this by guessing new codes.** The new identifiers were not retrievable in this
> environment — **the exact 2026 code strings are unverified.** Get the standards document, then
> update `docs/STANDARDS.md`. CLAUDE.md's "never invent a standards code" rule governs.

### 3.5 GAP #5 — AI literacy is entering **state law for middle school**

- **Utah** enacted **H.B. 218** (Digital Skills Amendments, signed **March 18** by Gov. Cox),
  updating the **required grades 7–8 digital skills course** to include news/AI literacy, and
  **H.B. 273**, requiring AI literacy woven through K–12. Districts have 2026–27 to adapt with
  **full implementation targeted for 2027–28**.
  https://newslit.org/news-and-research/utahs-digital-skills-course-gets-an-update-for-the-ai-age/ ·
  https://excelined.org/2026/05/26/state-k-12-ai-policy-in-2026-milestones/
- In the 2026 session, **77 bills across 27 states** addressed AI in classroom instruction, with
  ~10 enacted by mid-2026. https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/9/how-states-are-regulating-ai-in-education-this-legislative-session ·
  tracker: https://www.future-ed.org/legislative-tracker-2026-state-ai-in-education-bills/
- **Ohio** is the first state to require **every** district to adopt an AI policy, deadline
  **July 1, 2026** (HB 96) — note this mandates *policy*, **not** instruction.
  https://www.aiforeducation.io/blog/ohio-mandates-ai-policy-in-k12 ·
  https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/AI-in-Ohio-s-Education/Model-Policy
- Teacher-side readiness is weak, which is why turnkey material sells: by fall 2024, **67% of
  low-poverty districts** had provided teacher AI training vs **42% middle-poverty** and **39%
  high-poverty**. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA956-31.html

### 3.6 GAP #6 — Free curricula leave a **structural** hole that paid resources fill

NGPF's own content is free (including 60+ items free on TPT), and comparison writeups note that
neither NGPF nor EVERFI is a **full semester curriculum** — teachers piece lessons together and
supplement with assessments and deeper coverage to meet state requirements.
https://www.lyfecourse.com/pages/blog?p=everfi-vs-ngpf-vs-lyfe-course-financial-literacy-curriculum-comparison ·
https://rapunzl.com/blog/best-personal-finance-curriculum-high-school-2026
*(both are vendor-adjacent sources — competitors to NGPF — so read the framing critically; the
"not a full curriculum" point is consistent across both.)*

**Implication:** don't try to out-free the free stuff. Sell the **connective tissue** free curricula
lack for grades 6–8: assessments, exit tickets, sub-ready single periods, standards-citation pages
an admin will accept, and unplugged variants.

### 3.7 Where we should NOT expect a gap
- **Budgeting projects** (car/apartment/vacation/grocery). Saturated, with 200+-review incumbents.
- **Financial-literacy word searches / vocabulary puzzles.** Very high supply, low differentiation.
  (Many live TPT search paths exist: `financial+literacy+word+search`, `financial+literacy+terms`.)
- **Teacher-facing AI prompt libraries.** Multiple stores already there (MsReidTeach, MBazemore).
- **Lemonade stand / Shark Tank.** Universally covered.

## 7. So what for us — prioritized

### 1. Re-headline the AI line as **"no-device / unplugged AI literacy."** (Highest impact, most urgent)
The NYC 2K–8 AI ban is **one day old**, covers ~600k students, disables 38+ AI-enabled programs, and
caps grade 6–8 screen time at 45 min/day — while 73% of districts still teach AI literacy in 6–8.
Competitor flagships assume live model access. Rewrite the six AI product descriptions and the
bundle so "**no student AI accounts required · no devices · print and teach**" is in the **first
180 characters** and in the cover. Add "unplugged," "no devices," "no tech required," "screen-free"
to titles/tags where they fit. This is a positioning change, not a build.
*Sources: §3.1.*

### 2. Ship the finance line **now**, aimed at New York grade 8.
`finance-first-check`, `finance-risk-boss`, `finance-streamer-budget` are still `status: idea` while
the one mandate that hits grade 8 goes live this school year. Map each to NY's five strands
(budgeting & money management / credit & debt / earning income / **risk management** / saving &
investing), state the mandate language in the description, and note it works **embedded in an
existing course** — which is how NY lets districts deliver it. `finance-risk-boss` should be
**first**: "risk management" is a named NY strand and the TPT finance market is saturated with
budgeting instead. *Sources: §3.2, §4.*

### 3. Do **not** lead finance with another budget project.
Budgeting is the single most saturated concept in this niche (multiple "Budget Project" incumbents,
one bundle with 200+ 5-star reviews, plus free NGPF projects inside TPT search). Keep
`finance-streamer-budget` but position it on **income variability and creator economics**, not on
"make a budget." *Sources: §2.2, §3.6, §3.7.*

### 4. Raise the bundle prices and build a line bundle for every line.
Published norms put unit bundles at **$10–30+**; the AI mega-bundle signal in this niche is ~$46.
Six standards-aligned lessons at **$14** leaves money on the table and signals low value. Move
`bundle-ai-line` toward **$18–24** once it has ~10 reviews, and target **$12–18** for
`bundle-finance-line` at launch. Keep singles at $3–5 for findability, which is where discovery
happens. *Sources: §6.*

### 5. Promote the **games line** out of wave 2 — it's our least-contested category.
No dedicated grades 6–8 game-design store surfaced; supply is scattered worksheets and
device-dependent Scratch/Slides projects, while schools push esports/game design into middle grades
and buy commercial middle-school game-design electives. `games-paper-prototype` (freebie) and
`games-balance-math` are cheap for us and land in open water. Time the push to
**CSEdWeek, Dec 8–14 2026**, with an "Hour of Code with zero computers" angle.
*Sources: §2.4, §5.*

### 6. Add a middle-school **career-exposure** SKU to the entrepreneurship line, and aim it at Ohio.
Ohio requires grades 7–8 CTE exposure from **July 1, 2026**; every state now has some
middle-school career-exploration policy; PA requires a grade-8 career plan; UT/VA/ME have their own
requirements. Incumbent entrepreneurship supply is HS-CTE and digital-first. `ent-personal-brand`
already brushes this — reframe it explicitly as **career exploration / career-plan-ready** and pair
it with a print "career investigation" one-pager. *Sources: §3.3, §2.3.*

### 7. Fix the standards base before the next build: get the **2026 CSTA standards**.
CSTA's first revision since 2017 is out and now integrates AI, ethics and data. Our
`docs/STANDARDS.md` cites 2017 codes; so does every competitor. Obtain the standards document,
update the crosswalk, and only then use "2026 CSTA aligned" in copy. **The new code strings are
unverified — do not guess them** (CLAUDE.md rule). *Sources: §3.4.*

### 8. Compete on the connective tissue free curricula don't ship.
NGPF is free and *inside* TPT search, but neither NGPF nor EVERFI is a full curriculum — teachers
supplement with assessments and deeper coverage. Our differentiators should be the things they lack
at 6–8: exit tickets/rubrics in every product, single-period sub-ready lessons, an admin-facing
standards page, and unplugged variants. Also: at least one **freebie per line** is non-negotiable —
freebies are the documented mechanism for followers and first reviews, and **fewer than 10% of
buyers ever review**, so review accumulation is slow and must be started early.
*Sources: §3.6, https://sellerblog.teacherspayteachers.com/how-to-market-your-tpt-store/,
https://lindsaybowden.com/podcast/how-to-get-more-tpt-reviews/.*
