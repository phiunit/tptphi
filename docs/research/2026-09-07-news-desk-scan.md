## Market findings that change the plan (agent: market)
1. Teachers buy current-events resources steadily, but the reviews sit on ONE shape: an undated, reusable template pack
   that works with any story a teacher brings (a 15-template CNN 10 set at ~$3 carries ~1,544 reviews ≈ 15–30k sales at
   TPT's 5–10% review rate). Single "read this article, answer these questions" sheets are the commodity floor
   (free–$2.75, single-digit reviews). Year-long sets reach $14.99 (328 reviews); mega-bundles $25–$64 with few reviews.
2. Successful sellers refuse to sell dated content. Dated series fall under TPT's growing/progressive-bundle rules
   (must be titled "incomplete", list what arrives and when; TPT "may issue refunds on your behalf on a prorated basis").
   Real decay risk is pegging to a free source that changes (CNN 10 → The World from A to Z).
3. Copyright: "Your resources must only contain material that you own the rights to … or that you have the legal right to
   use commercially." No news carve-out. Compliant sellers ship the container (frame) + original passages. VOA text is
   public domain with credit, but VOA pages embed AP photos that are not — text only, never screenshots.
4. Calendar hooks we did not have: Constitution Day Sept 17 (federal instructional mandate), Digital Citizenship Week
   ≈ Oct 19–23 (verify), U.S. Media Literacy Week Oct 26–30 (NAMLE), Global MIL Week Oct 24–31, midterms Nov 3.
   September searches are routine searches ("bell work", "first week"), not topic searches.
5. AI-literacy need is documented (Media Literacy Now, Jan 2026: 25 states with media-literacy laws; EdWeek, Apr 2026:
   44% of middle-school educators say students struggle "a lot" to tell AI-generated from human content) but there is
   no visible TPT sales moat for "AI in the news" yet. Enter through the proven news-literacy FORMAT.
6. Headwind: Chalkbeat, Aug 3 2026 — TPT uses "algorithmic tools to identify and demote stores associated with
   low-quality, AI-generated content." Our human craft must be conspicuous on every surface.

## Verified but REJECTED by the lead (and why)
- Labor Day gas record ($4.14) and diesel record ($5.85): both hinge on an active war; the "cause" sentence is a political
  hot stove for a store whose brand is "safe to teach", and prices move weekly. Unit-rate math is better served by #5/#6.
- Teen unemployment 14.1% (BLS Sep 4): easy to misstate what a rate measures; coverage is partisan; skip at this band.
- GPT-6 Astra "Critical" cyber capability: the hook is the part that must be handled most carefully for 12-year-olds.
- X moves creator payouts in-house: platform reputation makes districts flinch at the name on a worksheet.
- Midterms (Nov 3) as a product hook: partisan by construction; our classroom-safe rule excludes it.
- Constitution Day variant (Sep 17): off our four lines, crowded shelf, 10 days out — not at our quality bar.
