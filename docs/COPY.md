# House copy standard — every word a buyer or a teacher reads

Applies to: TPT listing descriptions (`product.yaml`), UPLOAD_SHEET text, cover subs, unit overview,
and any teacher-facing prose. Student-page wording has its own rules (ANTI_SLOP 13b + the gloss rule).

## The diagnosis this standard fixes (2026-09-05)
Our listings are accurate and dense — and they read like a spec sheet. Three habits do the damage:
one 47-word sentence carrying hook + activity + mechanic; two or three em-dashes per paragraph (our own
ANTI_SLOP rule 10 bans stacked em-dashes, and the selling copy breaks it); and an INCLUDES paragraph that
is a 130-word run-on inventory where a scannable list belongs.

**Not the fix: making listings shorter.** Length and keyword coverage are how TPT search finds us. Same
length, better shape.

## The shape (every lesson listing, in this order)
1. **Filter line.** `NO STUDENT AI ACCOUNTS NEEDED. This lesson runs on paper.` Nothing else on that line.
2. **Hook line.** The analogy in one sentence, ≤ 16 words, present tense.
3. **Do line.** What the kids actually do, naming the activity, ≤ 22 words.
4. **How it runs.** One paragraph, 3–5 sentences, the 45 minutes in order. Keyword-rich: the lesson's
   search terms live here (AI literacy, prompt writing, fact-checking, middle school…).
5. **WHAT YOU GET.** One line per file, each starting `• `, each naming the page/slide count and the one
   thing that makes it worth paying for.
6. **Standards:** one line, codes exactly as the yaml.
7. **Unit line.** Lesson N of 6, stands alone, bundle saves 30%, the store search phrase.

## The rules
- **One idea per sentence.** Average 15 words, cap 25. After a long sentence, land a short one.
- **Max one em-dash per paragraph.** Never two in one sentence. A period usually beats a dash.
- **Second person, present tense, active.** "Your students fix three cursed prompts", not "students will
  be asked to repair prompts". The teacher is "you". Never "the educator", never "learners".
- **Verbs over nouns.** "They swap sheets and break each other's wishes", not "an adversarial peer-review
  activity is included".
- **No colon-stacking.** One colon per paragraph, none in the first three lines.
- **Say the number.** "2 pages", "11 slides", "three passages", "45 minutes". Numerals, not words.
- **Every claim checkable.** If a page count or a promise changes, the copy changes. Hedge the future
  ("so far", "nobody can promise") — never hedge what's in the file.
- **Read it aloud.** Any sentence you can't say in one breath is two sentences.
- **No AI-tell phrasing.** delve, dive in, unlock, elevate, in today's fast-paced, it's important to note,
  transformative, seamless, robust, comprehensive suite.

## Reference — Lesson 1, before and after
BEFORE (one 47-word sentence, three em-dashes):
> The hook: AI is a literal genie that grants what you SAID, not what you MEANT — so in the Cursed Wish
> Repair Shop, students rewrite three kids' cursed prompts, then swap sheets and play the Worst Genie Ever
> to twist each other's.

AFTER (three lines, one breath each):
> AI is a literal genie. It grants what you SAID, not what you MEANT.
> Your students repair three cursed prompts in the Cursed Wish Repair Shop.
> Then they swap sheets and play the Worst Genie Ever, twisting each other's wishes to find the holes.

BEFORE (INCLUDES as a 130-word run-on) → AFTER:
> **WHAT YOU GET**
> • Lesson plan, 2 pages. Every block timed to 45 minutes, every transition scripted, a what-if under each.
> • Cursed Wish Repair Shop worksheet, 2 pages with exit ticket. Plus a fillable PDF for Chromebooks and tablets.
> • Teacher guide, 3 pages. A key or exemplar for every student item, Worst-Genie improv tips, an AI-honesty
>   discussion guide, and a when-it-goes-sideways list.
> • Slide deck, 11 slides (.pptx). Teacher script and answer keys in the speaker notes.
> • Support, Extension, ELL and no-device variants.
> Zero prep. Print it tonight, teach it tomorrow.
