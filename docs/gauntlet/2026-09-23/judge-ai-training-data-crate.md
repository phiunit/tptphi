## Verdict

This is a four-star lesson with a five-star idea. The AutoMix bit is great teaching. "Grandma loves bass drops, probably," the imaginary aux cord, and Cool Down (Kinda) set up the Nia/Keisha argument on purpose, and "name the people, not just the songs" puts 2-IC-21 into seven words a kid can act on. A teacher can print it and run it. Four things stand between it and "best resource I bought this year."
1. **The assessed back half is undersized and repeats itself.** Exit #3 asks for the same gap-plus-add the student wrote on the Repeat, Then Add lines ten minutes earlier. Every exit answer gets 2 lines when the key's own exemplar needs 3–4. About 84 words of writing are due in 5 minutes.
2. **The analogy's middle step never happens.** PATTERNS is the blank the exit ticket assesses, but the game has kids match a request to a printed list. That is a lookup, and the direct instruction says the AI makes "a prediction, not a lookup."
3. **Peer steps name no first speaker** (ANTI_SLOP 14): no rule for who is Partner A, and no order for the say-back or for the pair-to-pair live request.
4. **The deck has three problems.** It shows students the warm-up punchlines before the teacher says them. One exit question on the slide is worded differently from the worksheet. And it states a research finding more strongly than the teacher guide does, on a slide students read.

Each fix is small, and none of them needs a new page.
