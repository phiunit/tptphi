## Verdict

This is a four-star product that is close to five. The core is excellent. The warm-up trap works: a smooth paragraph, a class vote, then a half-truth reveal ("Venus really is the hottest"). Draft #1's "true claim, false reason" ending is sharp teaching for RI.x.8. The fabricated Edison quote is flagged in the key as written for the exercise. The exemplar notes are sized to the four ruled lines, and the teacher lines are ones a teacher would actually say ("Don't ask the suspect if they're innocent", "AI is autocomplete with confidence", "Presses roll in five!"). What would cost stars is classroom mechanics and consistency, not substance.
- The teacher has to model underlining on a paragraph students never see.
- One of the worksheet's ranking rules ("Mark S when everybody already knows it") waves through the lesson's own planted Sydney error.
- Partner A is never defined.
- The "verdicts out loud" step calls on one desk per stamp before anyone has counted which stamps were given.
- The AI mechanism is stated as a universal ("it does not check them", "never checks them"), which breaks the house hedging rule (ANTI_SLOP 17), and a student who uses a search-enabled chatbot will push back on it.
- The key contradicts itself on Draft #3 ("KILL" vs "the true facts survive" vs "nothing left") and on how to score "Google it".
- The culture lens (hip-hop/gaming/anime as the thing that carries the concept) is almost absent. The only nods are the decorative "boss level" and "sus".

None of these is a blocker. All of them are cheap to fix.

## Top 5 moves

1. **Give the teacher a visible paragraph to model on.** LP:109 and deck notes :35 say "model steps 1–2 on the warm-up paragraph", but the paragraph only exists in the teacher's script and never appears on a slide or page. Slides 6 and 7 make the same point twice (slides.yaml:36–44). Keep slide 6 (compare) with the title changed to "Don't ask the suspect if they're innocent." Repurpose slide 7 as **"The warm-up, corrected"**: the five sentences with the two errors struck through and corrected inline ("~~Venus~~ **Mercury** is closest…", "~~the only planet~~ **the planet best known for** rings"). No uncorrected false claim is shown, so the Gate 0 rule holds. The deck stays at 12 slides. Change LP:109 to: "Put the four steps up (slide 5), then the corrected warm-up (slide 7). Model steps 1 and 2 out loud: underline five claims, then mark each C or S."
2. **Fix the S rule that lets Sydney through.** WS:129 "Mark S when everybody already knows it.", LP:112 "Common knowledge → S (safe).", and slides.yaml:32 "Everybody knows it? Mark S." all teach the exact trap Draft #2 plants, since "everybody knows" Sydney is the capital. Replace them with WS:129 "Mark **S** only when it's low risk: not a record, not a number, and the ending doesn't need it. 'Everybody knows' is how Sydney sneaks in.", LP:112 "Low risk (not a record, not a number, the ending doesn't lean on it) → S (safe). 'Everybody knows' is not a reason.", and slide card "You can't check everything. Would it hurt if wrong? Mark C. Low risk? Mark S."
3. **Make the pair and round mechanics traceable (ANTI_SLOP 14).** LP:125 should read: "Round change. Partner A is the partner whose first name comes first in the alphabet. A, take Theo's draft on page 2. B, take Marcus's draft on page 3. Six minutes on your own sheet. Pick your stamp and write your note before the swap." Put the same A rule on slide 8. In LP:126, the 2 minutes after the swap become "Owners take their sheet back and fix their note if the new claim changes it." A 22-word note at 10–13 words a minute (1.7–2.2 min) no longer has to fit into the last 2 minutes. Reorder LP:129 so the count comes before the call (text in Lens 1, L13).
4. **Hedge the AI mechanism once, reuse the sentence everywhere, and let the class feel it.**
   - The master line is "AI writes by predicting likely words. It doesn't always check them." It goes in Deja's note (WS:67), the bridge (LP:97, slides.yaml:21), the exit key (TG:133), the deck subtitle (slides.yaml:5), and the listing hook if you choose (product.yaml:24).
   - Add a what-if at LP:107: *If a student says "my AI shows links":* "Good. Click one. A link counts as a second source only if you open it and it says the same thing."
   - Inside the 3-minute "why it happens" block, add a 60-second crowd-autocomplete game. The class becomes the model: "Finish my sentence: 'Twinkle, twinkle, little…'" (star). "'Bulls get mad when they see the color…'" (red). "You just did what AI does. You predicted the likely word. Bulls barely see red; it's the moving cape." The analogy then does the teaching, not just the decorating.
5. **Make the teacher guide agree with itself before a buyer spots it.**
   - Draft #3: TG:102 should read "Verdict: **KILL**. The lead claim is false and its proof is invented, so the story goes. The phonograph and Wright facts are true and could start a new story." TG:111 should read "…Without the telephone and the quote, the ending has no support."
   - "Google it" scoring: TG:123 gives no credit and TG:134 gives partial. Pick one (partial) and state it the same way in both places.
   - Add a C/S grading line to TG p2: "Must be C: the planted errors and each ending. Any other C/S split earns credit if the desk can say why." Move the TG p3 "true claims" note (TG:120) up under the p2 key so the key reads in one place.
