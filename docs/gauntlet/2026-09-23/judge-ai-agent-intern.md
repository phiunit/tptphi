# Judge report: ai-agent-intern ("Lead Your AI Squad", L6 of 6, AI Literacy)

Judged 2026-09-23 by a fresh critic. Sources read: every PNG in `dist/review/` (cover, 2 plan pages, 3 worksheet pages, 3 guide pages, slides 01–12), pymupdf text of every shipped PDF plus the fillable's widget map, the Preview PDF, the zip (checked byte-for-byte against dist: in sync), `src/*.html`, `src/slides.yaml` with notes, `product.yaml`, `UPLOAD_SHEET.md`. Readability gate re-run: worksheet FK 4.5 (pass). Blindness kept: no JUDGE.md, no other gauntlet files, no git history.

## Verdict

This is a strong lesson with a real idea behind it. The gaming frame does real work: the painfully literal party member is the AI, the quest card is the brief, and checking the loot is the review. The teacher lines are quotable. "You'll never out-work it. You CAN out-lead it." "'Make it better' is banned." "A guess wearing a costume." "A RUN IT BACK on the leader." Those sell the product. It is not five-star yet, because the core activity has plumbing gaps that a tired teacher will hit live. Nobody is told where the quest card sits during Step 3. Two of the three roles only copied GOAL and DONE LOOKS LIKE, yet their jobs need WHAT WE KNOW and RULES. Role assignment and the 60-second read-back have no turn order (ANTI_SLOP 14). In Step 4, the worksheet and the deck give different evidence rules. The DONE-NESS check measures a one-role slice against the whole quest. The KEEP/FIX and evidence boxes hold about half of what the key's exemplar needs (ANTI_SLOP 15). The worst problem is in the teacher guide. Its filled CHECK row "quotes" things DeShawn's exemplar plan never says, so the answer key models fake evidence in the lesson that teaches checking evidence. There is also a thesis gap. The lesson claims agents "fill the gap with a guess and keep going", but the unplugged agents are told never to guess. On paper, the loot never has a cursed item in it for the leader to catch. Fix the plumbing and plant one guess, and this becomes the best lesson in the unit.

## Top 5 moves

1. **Put the card where everyone can see it, and say who does what.** Add one sentence to worksheet Step 2 (l.94), plan Activity 1 (l.114) and slide 9 item 1: "Decide together; the card-holder writes. The card-holder is the Planner. Of the other two, the first name in ABC order is the Scout." Add to worksheet Step 3 (l.109), plan l.116 Switch and slide 9 item 2: "The card-holder's page 1 goes face-up in the middle of the triangle. Read it all you want. Ask nothing." Also move "Sit in a triangle…" from the swap line into the Switch line, so seats are set before the silent round and not at minute 16. Then either drop "the other two copy GOAL and DONE LOOKS LIKE" on every surface, or keep it and justify it ("so you can check done-ness later").
2. **Make Step 4 one clear procedure, and size it honestly.** (a) Pick one evidence rule and print it on every surface. Recommended: "Quote evidence for EVERY row." That matches the table, slide 10 and the key, so shorten the KEEP/FIX demand to match the time. The other option is "for the RUN IT BACK row only", which means greying the evidence cells on SHIP IT rows. (b) DONE-NESS becomes "did they do every job on their role card?" (worksheet l.124, slide 10 bullet 3, plan l.98). (c) Name the note one way everywhere: **keep-and-fix note** (listing already uses it). Worksheet l.127, plan l.98, slide 4 and slide 10 currently say run-it-back note / keep + fix / keep/fix note. (d) Read-back order: "Last 60 seconds: the Scout reads first, then the Critic, then the Planner. 20 seconds each." (e) Give the evidence cells 4 ruled lines (the key's TASTE evidence is 86 characters against about 42 of space). Give KEEP and FIX 5 lines each (the key's FIX is 95 characters against about 66). There is room: page 2 has about 15% free and page 3 has about 30%.
3. **Plant the guess so the loot has something to catch.** The lesson's thesis is "the agent usually fills the gap with a guess and keeps going". Paper agents are told "don't guess, write NOT ON THE CARD", so ACCURACY almost always ships. Add one zero-prep line to plan Activity 2 and the slide 9 notes: "At minute 2, whisper to one Scout per team: 'Make up ONE fact that sounds true and mark it ✓ anyway.' That's the cursed amulet in the loot. Leaders, find it." It costs nothing and uses the warm-up's own cursed-item joke. It makes ACCURACY the most exciting row and turns "check the loot" into a real hunt. Also swap one warm-up order for a guess-forward one. See Lens 1, lesson-plan l.84–86. Change it on both the plan and slide 2.
4. **Make the answer key true to its own exemplar.** Rewrite the Filled CHECK row (guide l.78–80, slide 10 notes l.73) so every quote appears in DeShawn's plan (guide l.70). Add the Scout's missing 4–8-minute output, the written questions (guide l.71). Reconcile the slogan "If you redo a party member's work, your quest card failed" with the exemplar, which RUN IT BACKs DeShawn when the card was clear. Replacement lines are in Lens 1.
5. **Sell it from the preview and the first two listing lines.** The Preview PDF shows worksheet p.1 (mostly blank ruled lines) and guide p.3. Swap them for **worksheet p.2** (role cards plus the CHECK rubric, the page that makes a teacher say "oh, that's clever") and **guide p.2** (Sana's squad exemplar). Rewrite the listing hook at l.26. "Guesses forward" is unclear, and "your card" means nothing to a buyer on line 2. Proposed: "An AI agent runs the whole quest alone, and it guesses wherever your orders are vague." (16 words.) Then run the consistency sweep in Lens 1: exit ticket "it" (all surfaces), "said yes to it" → "them", the plan and deck bridge lines, and "Your" vs "My" party member.
