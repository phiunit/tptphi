Why This Lesson

When AI can generate anything, the skill that still separates students is taste — knowing what's good, knowing what's them, and being able to say it precisely. This lesson turns that into a routine: the Taste File makes a student's style visible, and the editor loop (draft yourself → seek specific feedback → revise yourself) is the writing-process standard W.6.5 in miniature — with the classroom AI policy built in: AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter.

Coaching the "AI Editor" Role

The partner playing the AI has three rules, and holding them IS the rigor: use only the taste file (if a note can't point at a section, cut it), exactly 2 notes (editors prioritize), and notes, not rewrites (the second the editor writes a sentence, they've become a ghostwriter).

Post this sentence frame: "Your taste file says ______ (section #), but ______ reads ______. Try ______." A good note names the gap AND leaves the fix to the writer.

Watch for the classic slip: partners "helping" by rewriting. Redirect with: "You're the editor. Point at the taste file and hand the pen back."

Exemplar Taste-File Entries

There is no single right taste — score for specificity. Entries at this level are the target:

StudentVibe + pet peeve"So me" sentence
Jaylenfunny, blunt, lowkey. "Never use words I'd have to look up to sound smart.""Saturday I made the world's saddest pancake and ate it anyway."
Priyadramatic, poetic, big idea first. "Don't shorten my sentences — I like them long on purpose.""The weekend arrived like a plot twist nobody asked for, and I loved every second."
Tomáshype, short + punchy, lists. "No baby-talk. I'm 12, not 5.""Game. Winner. Rematch. That was the whole weekend."

Scoring Guidance

  • Proficient: taste file is specific enough that a stranger could imitate the student; both notes point at sections; revision visibly moves the draft toward the file, changes starred.
  • Developing: taste file is real but vague ("funny, good"); notes are generic; revision changes words without changing voice.
  • Beginning: taste file restates the word bank; notes rewrite instead of noting; revision is the editor's sentence, not the writer's.

With Devices — The Copy-Ready AI Feedback Prompt

In Activity 2, Step 2, students with devices upgrade their editor from a partner to a real AI tool. They type (or paste) this, filling the brackets from their own worksheet:

"Here is my taste file: [read in your 5 sections]. Here is my 3–4 sentence draft: [your draft]. You are my editor, not my ghostwriter. Give me exactly 2 specific notes on where the draft does not match my taste file, and say which section each note points at. Do not rewrite anything."

Debrief question for device classes: "Which editor gave the more usable note — your partner or the AI? What did the winner know?" The answer is always the same: whoever read the taste file more closely.

What a Good Note Looks Like

Ghost-note (reject)Editor's note (accept)
"Make it funnier. Maybe say: 'Welcome to my chaotic channel!'""Taste file section 1 says funny + blunt, but sentence 3 is a plain fact list. Where's the joke?"
"It's good, I like it.""Section 3 says no textbook voice — 'I enjoy various hobbies' is textbook voice. Which hobby, said your way?"

Full Standards Text

  • CCSS ELA W.6.5 — With some guidance and support from peers and adults, develop and strengthen writing as needed by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach. Taught: Direct Instruction (the editor loop) + Activity 2, The Editor's Chair. Assessed: Step 3 starred revision + Exit Ticket.
  • ISTE 1.6.b — Students create original works or responsibly repurpose or remix digital resources into new creations. Taught: Activity 1, Build Your Taste File + Activity 2, Step 1 draft. Assessed: Editor's Loop Checklist rows 1 and 4 (original draft, own revision).
  • ISTE 1.1.c — Students use technology to seek feedback that informs and improves their practice. Taught: Activity 2, Step 2 (students hand their taste file to an AI — or a partner playing one — and request 2 specific notes). Assessed: Editor's Loop Checklist rows 2 and 3 + the Note 1/Note 2 records.

Support — Editor Frames & Tone Words

Extra tone words: honest · goofy · calm · fierce · curious · sharp · warm · spooky.

Note frame: "Your taste file says ____ (section #), but ____ reads ____. Try ____."

Revision frame: "I changed ____ to ____ because my taste file says ____."
