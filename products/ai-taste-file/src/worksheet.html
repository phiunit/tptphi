The Editor's Chair

The loop: YOU draft → your editor gives notes → YOU revise. Two roles: the Writer is you, on your own sheet. The Editor is your partner playing the AI (or a real AI): reads your taste file, gives 2 notes, never rewrites.

Step 1 — Draft It Yourself

Write 3–4 sentences: the About Me for a channel or profile page you would really make. Make it match your taste file. No AI, no partner.

Step 2 — The Editor's Notes

Swap sheets with your partner — all pages. You are now the EDITOR for their draft. Read THEIR taste file (page 1). Read THEIR draft. Then write 2 notes on THEIR sheet, in the boxes below. Each note names a taste-file section (1–5) and says what doesn't match it. Notes, not rewrites — never write their sentence for them. With devices: your teacher types one taste file and draft into an AI on the big screen, and the class checks its 2 notes.

Then talk — 1 minute. Editor: read both notes out loud. Writer: ask ONE question ("Which sentence?" or "What would fit?"). Editor: answer using only the taste file. Then swap back.

Revise & Last Check

Sheets swapped back? Good. From here on, this page is yours — your draft, your pen.

Step 3 — Revise It Yourself

Rewrite your draft below, using your editor's two notes. Star (*) every change you made. Your editor does not write a single word for you.

Editor's Loop Checklist — the loop, done right✓
I wrote the first draft myself — no AI, no copying.
Each note names a taste-file section and says what to change — not "make it better."
I rewrote it myself and starred every change.
We talked it through: my editor read both notes out loud, I asked one question, they answered from my taste file.

Last Check

1. One change I made (the old words → the new words):

2. It fits my taste file because (name the section):

3. Why would the note "make it better" NOT have helped you?

Be honest with yourself: if the editor writes your sentence, your name is on words you never wrote — and you'd know it. Your draft, your pen, your taste.
