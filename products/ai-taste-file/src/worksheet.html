The Editor's Chair

The loop: YOU draft → your editor gives notes → YOU revise. You are the writer. Your editor is your partner, playing the AI. They read your taste file, give 2 notes, and never rewrite.

Step 1 — Draft It Yourself

Write 3–4 sentences: the About Me for a channel or profile page you would really make. Make it match your taste file. No AI, no partner.

Step 2 — The Editor's Notes

Swap ALL pages with your partner. Now you are the EDITOR. (1) Read their taste file on page 1. (2) Read their draft. (3) Write 2 notes in the boxes below. Each note names a section (1–5) and says what doesn't match it. Notes only. Never write their sentence for them.

A good note sounds like this: "Section 1 says funny and blunt — sentence 2 sounds like a homework robot. Say it like you'd text a friend."

Note 1 — points at taste-file section #

Note 2 — points at taste-file section #

Then Talk — 2 Minutes

Speaker 1 is the partner whose first name comes first in the alphabet. Speaker 1 is the editor for the first minute: read your two notes out loud. Writer: ask ONE question. Editor: answer from THEIR taste file — never your own taste. Then switch: Speaker 2 is the editor for minute two. Then swap sheets back.
The writer's question: "Which sentence?" or "What would fit?"

Revise & Last Check

Sheets swapped back? Good. From here on, this page is yours — your draft, your pen.

Step 3 — Revise It Yourself

Copy your draft below. Change only the sentences your notes point at, plus any word that has to change with them. Star (*) every change. Your editor does not write a single word for you.

Editor Loop Checklist✓
I wrote the first draft myself — no AI, no copying.
My editor's notes each named a section and said what to change, not "make it better."
I made the changes myself and starred every one.
My editor read both notes aloud, I asked one question, and they answered from my taste file.

Last Check

1. The one word or phrase you changed (old → new):

2. The new version fits my taste file because (name the section):

3. Why would the note "make it better" NOT have helped you?

Be honest with yourself: if the editor writes your sentence, your name is on words you never wrote — and you'd know it. Your draft, your pen, your taste.
