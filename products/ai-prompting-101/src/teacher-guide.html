Teacher Guide & Answer Key

How to Talk to AI — Worst-Genie improv tips, sample repairs, scoring guidance, and the AI-honesty discussion.

Playing the Worst Genie Ever

Three moves cover every wish: take a word literally ("rich" = a name), fill the missing detail badly (money = Monopoly money), or grant it in the least useful form (a story = one word: "Hero."). Stay cheerful — the genie is never mean, just maddeningly literal. The gold moment is when a student adds fine print and you have to grant it properly: applaud it.

Sample Genie-Proof Repairs

There is no single right answer — score with the Genie-Proof Checklist. Exemplars:

CursedExemplar repair
"tell me about space""I'm a 7th grader making a one-page poster about black holes. Give me 5 amazing facts, each under 20 words, written so a 5th grader could get it. No math formulas."
"help me save money""I'm 13 and get $20 a week. I want $150 in 3 months for sneakers. Make a simple weekly savings plan as a table, and give 2 tips for not spending on snacks."
"make a story about a hero""Write the opening paragraph (max 100 words) of an anime-style story about a shy kid whose sketchbook drawings come to life. Mysterious tone, end on a cliffhanger."

Scoring Guidance

  • Proficient: all three P.R.O. clauses present and specific; partner-genie couldn't twist it.
  • Developing: two clauses, or vague ones ("make it short").
  • Beginning: restated the cursed wish with more words.

The AI-Honesty Discussion (use anytime it comes up)

Q: "Is using AI cheating?" — Frame: using AI to brainstorm, get feedback, or check understanding = consulting the genie. Handing in the genie's work with your name on it = a wish you never earned. Ask: "What did YOU make?"

Q: "Is the AI always right?" — No. It predicts likely words; it can be confidently wrong ("hallucinate") — a trickster genie. Rule of thumb: anything that's a checkable fact (dates, numbers, names, quotes) gets checked against a real source before you use it.

Q: "Will AI take all the jobs?" — The honest answer: it changes jobs. The people who win are the ones who can direct it well — which is exactly the skill in today's lesson.
