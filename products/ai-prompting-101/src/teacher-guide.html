Teacher Guide & Answer Key

How to Talk to AI — Worst-Genie improv tips, sample repairs, scoring guidance, and the AI-honesty discussion.

Why This Lesson

Every AI job runs human → AI → human: a person starts it (the prompt) and a person ends it (the check). Those two human jobs are the whole skill, and they transfer to every AI tool your students will ever touch. This lesson trains both: P.R.O. is the start; the trickster-genie habit is the end.

Playing the Worst Genie Ever

Three moves cover every wish: take a word literally ("rich" = a name), fill the missing detail badly (money = play money), or grant it in the least useful form (a story = one word: "Hero."). Stay cheerful — the genie is never mean, just maddeningly literal. The gold moment is when a student adds fine print and you have to grant it properly: applaud it.

Sample Genie-Proof Repairs

There is no single right answer — score with the Genie-Proof Checklist. Exemplars:

CursedExemplar repair
Amara — "tell me about space""I'm a 7th grader making a one-page science poster about black holes, due Friday. Give me 5 amazing facts, each under 20 words, written so a 5th grader could get it. No math formulas."
Mateo — "help me save money""I'm 12 and get $15 a week. I want $120 headphones in 10 weeks. Make a simple weekly savings plan as a table, and give 2 tips for not spending it on snacks."
Yuki — "make a story about a hero""Write the opening paragraph (max 100 words) of an anime-style story about a shy kid whose sketchbook drawings come to life. Mysterious tone, end on a cliffhanger."

Scoring Guidance

  • Proficient: all three P.R.O. clauses present and specific; partner-genie couldn't twist it.
  • Developing: two clauses, or vague ones ("make it short").
  • Beginning: restated the cursed wish with more words.

The AI-Honesty Discussion (use anytime it comes up)

Q: "Is using AI cheating?" — One sentence settles it: AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter. Feedback, brainstorming, checking understanding = editor. Handing in its work with your name on it = ghostwriter, and a wish you never earned. Ask: "What did YOU make?"

Q: "Is the AI always right?" — No. It predicts likely words; it can be confidently wrong ("hallucinate") — a trickster genie. Rule of thumb: anything that's a checkable fact (dates, numbers, names, quotes) gets checked against a real source before you use it.

Q: "Will AI take all the jobs?" — The honest answer: it changes jobs. The people who win are the ones who can direct it well — which is exactly the skill in today's lesson.

Support: P.R.O. Sentence Starters

P: "I am a ____ grader working on ____. I want ____ for ____."

R: "Keep it ____ (length). Use a ____ tone. Include ____. Do NOT ____."

O: "Give me the answer as a ____ (list / table / paragraph / steps)."
