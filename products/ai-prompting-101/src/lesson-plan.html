How to Talk to AI

45 minutes, no prep. The hook: AI is a literal genie — it grants what you SAID, not what you MEANT. Students learn both human jobs in every AI task: the prompt and the check. Stands alone.

Warm-Up — The Worst Genie Ever (8 min)

You are a genie who grants wishes exactly as worded. Take 3–4 wishes and ruin every one, cheerfully:

"I wish I was rich." → "Done. Your name is now Rich."
"I wish for a million dollars." → "Granted — one million dollars, in play money."
"I wish school was over." → "Granted. The school building is now over... there."
Then ask: "Whose fault was that — the genie's, or the wish?" Bridge: "AI works like that genie. It doesn't know what you MEANT — it only knows what you SAID. Every AI job goes human, AI, human: you start it with the wish, the genie does the middle, and you check what comes back. Today you get good at both human parts."

What if a wish is inappropriate? Don't play it: "The genie doesn't grant that one. Next wish." Nobody volunteers? Wish first yourself: "I wish for a snack" → "Granted: one grain of rice."

Transition: "So how do you write a wish a genie can't twist? Lawyers figured this out a long time ago."

Direct Instruction — Parts of a Genie-Proof Wish (10 min)

Lawyers make contracts genie-proof with clauses. Strong prompts have three parts — write P.R.O. on the board as you go:

P — Purpose. What you want + who it's for. "Explain photosynthesis to a 6th grader" beats "explain photosynthesis."
R — Requirements. The rules: length, tone, format, include / leave out. This is what stops the twist.
O — Output. What should the finished answer look like? A list? A paragraph? A table? A plan?
Watch one get fixed — build the repair on the board, naming each part as you add it. Cursed wish: "help with my history essay" → Genie-proof P.R.O. prompt: "I'm writing a 7th grade history essay on the printing press. Give me 3 thesis statements and 2 pieces of evidence for each, as a bullet list. Don't write it for me."

Direct Instruction (cont.)

Say it plainly: "See the last sentence — don't write it for me. AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter. It gives a second opinion on YOUR work; it doesn't hand in work with your name on it."

What if someone says the fixed prompt is "too long"? Count it: three sentences. "Three sentences now, or forty pages of NASA history later. Your call."

Hand-out / transition: "Your turn. Page 1: three kids typed cursed wishes. Fix all three — every part, P, R and O, every time. Solo. Ten minutes."

Activity — Cursed Wish Repair Shop (20 min)

  1. Repair (10 min): Solo, students fix Amara's, Mateo's and Yuki's cursed wishes on page 1 with all three P.R.O. parts. Circulate; read a stuck student's repair aloud in a genie voice.
    What if a student writes nearly the same repair three times? "What does Mateo need that Yuki doesn't?" A plan is not a story — O must change. Finished early? Extension below.
    Swap: "Pens down. Swap sheets with your partner. You are now the Worst Genie Ever, and page 2 is where you do your damage."
  2. Break it (7 min): Pairs swap sheets and play the Worst Genie Ever on page 2 of their PARTNER's sheet: say a twist out loud for each wish, check the boxes on the Genie-Proof Checklist, write the best twist, swap back. Twistable = needs more rules.
    What if a partner checks every box without trying? Make them say the twist out loud. Can't twist it — the ✓ stands. Can twist it — uncheck it and write the twist.
    Swap back: "Sheets back with their owners. Genies, point at the ONE wish on your partner's sheet you could NOT twist. That's the one we want to hear."
  3. Showcase (3 min): 2–3 pairs read a "cursed → genie-proof" before-and-after. With devices: run the best one live and critique what comes back.
    What if the live AI answer is bad anyway? Perfect — "Which part of the wish let it do that?" The class adds a rule and you run it again.
    Into the exit ticket: "Your OWN sheet, bottom of page 2. Before you write, one more rule about genies."

Exit Ticket — Last Check (7 min: 1 + 6)

First minute — the trickster rule: "Genies are tricksters. AI can grant your wish with made-up facts, and sound sure. Anything checkable — a name, a date, a number — gets checked somewhere real before you use it." Six minutes, solo: #1 a genie-proof prompt for another class + what the genie would hand back for just its first five words; #2 the printed genie answer to one class's repaired wish (about black holes) — which sentence to double-check, and where. Collect; key in the Teacher Guide only.

What if a student says "it all sounds right"? "Which sentence has a number or a date in it? Numbers and dates are checkable. Start there."

Differentiation

  • Support: P.R.O. sentence starters (Teacher Guide); repair two wishes instead of three.
  • Extension: Write a wish that CAN'T be genie-proofed in under 50 words — then explain why.
  • ELL: The sentence starters double as language frames; draft in the strongest language first, then translate.
  • No-device classrooms: Built in — the teacher and partners ARE the genie. Skip the "with devices" lines.
