How to Talk to AI

A 45-minute prompt-writing lesson. The hook: AI is a literal genie — it grants exactly what your words ask for, not what your brain meant. Students learn to write genie-proof prompts.

Objectives

  • Students can explain why the same AI gives different quality answers to different prompts.
  • Students can apply the P.R.O. framework (Purpose, Requirements, Output) to write a clear prompt.
  • Students can evaluate an AI response for accuracy and identify when to double-check it.

Warm-Up — The Worst Genie Ever (7 min)

Announce that you are a genie, and you grant wishes exactly as worded. Take 3–4 wishes from the class and ruin every one, cheerfully:

"I wish I was rich." → "Done. Your name is now Rich."

"I wish for a million dollars." → "Granted — one million dollars, in Monopoly money."

"I wish school was over." → "Granted. The school building is now over... there."

Then ask: whose fault was that — the genie's, or the wish?

Bridge: "AI works like that genie. It doesn't know what you meant — it only knows what you SAID. It grants your words, not your thoughts. Today you learn to write wishes with fine print."

Direct Instruction — The Three Clauses of a Genie-Proof Wish (8 min)

Lawyers make contracts genie-proof with clauses. Strong prompts have three — write P.R.O. on the board:

P — Purpose. What you want + who it's for. "Explain photosynthesis to a 6th grader" beats "explain photosynthesis."

R — Requirements. The fine print: length, tone, format, include/avoid. This is what stops the genie from twisting your wish.

O — Output. What should the granted wish look like? A list? A paragraph? A table? A plan?

Model it live (on the board or with a projected AI tool):

Cursed wishGenie-proof P.R.O. prompt
"help with my history essay""I'm writing a 5-paragraph essay for 7th grade history on why the printing press mattered. Give me 3 possible thesis statements and 2 pieces of evidence for each, as a bullet list. Don't write the essay for me."
Say it plainly: "See the last clause — don't write the essay for me. If the genie writes it, it was never your wish. Use AI to think WITH you, not FOR you."

Activity — Cursed Wish Repair Shop (20 min)

  1. Repair (8 min): Solo, students rewrite the three cursed wishes on the worksheet using all three P.R.O. clauses.
  2. Break it (7 min): Pairs swap sheets and play the Worst Genie Ever: misread each repaired wish as literally as possible, then score it with the Genie-Proof Checklist. Twistable = needs more fine print.
  3. Showcase (5 min): 2–3 students read a "cursed → genie-proof" before-and-after. With devices: run the best one live and critique what comes back.

Exit Ticket (5 min)

Bottom of worksheet: students write one genie-proof prompt they could actually use this week for another class, and name one thing they'd double-check in the AI's answer before trusting it.

Differentiation

  • Support: Provide the P.R.O. sentence starters (Teacher Guide, p.2).
  • Extension: Students write a wish that CAN'T be genie-proofed in under 50 words, then explain why specificity has limits.
  • No-device classrooms: Built in — the teacher and partners ARE the genie.
