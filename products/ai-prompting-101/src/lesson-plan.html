Prompt Like a Producer

A 45-minute AI literacy lesson: students learn that the quality of an AI's answer depends on the quality of the direction it gets — just like a track depends on its producer.

Objectives

  • Students can explain why the same AI gives different quality answers to different prompts.
  • Students can apply the P.R.O. framework (Purpose, Requirements, Output) to write a clear prompt.
  • Students can evaluate an AI response for accuracy and identify when to double-check it.

Warm-Up — "Make It Good" (5 min)

Ask a volunteer to stand up. Tell them: "Sing something. Make it good." Let the awkwardness land, then ask the class: why was that hard? (No genre, no mood, no song, no direction.)

Bridge: "In a studio, the producer's whole job is direction — genre, tempo, mood, reference tracks. Today you learn to be the producer. The AI is the artist in the booth waiting for direction."

Direct Instruction — The P.R.O. Framework (10 min)

Write on the board and unpack with the class:

P — Purpose. What do you actually want, and who is it for? "Explain photosynthesis to a 6th grader" beats "explain photosynthesis."

R — Requirements. The creative direction: length, tone, format, what to include, what to avoid. This is the producer's genre-and-tempo call.

O — Output. What should the final product look like? A list? A paragraph? A table? A song with a hook?

Model it live (on the board or with a projected AI tool):

Weak promptP.R.O. prompt
"help with my history essay""I'm writing a 5-paragraph essay for 7th grade history on why the printing press mattered. Give me 3 possible thesis statements and 2 pieces of evidence for each, as a bullet list. Don't write the essay for me."
Say it plainly: "Notice the last line — don't write the essay for me. Using AI to think WITH you is a skill. Turning in its work as yours is just letting somebody else rap over your name."

Activity — Prompt Remix Studio (20 min)

  1. Solo (8 min): Students remix the three weak prompts on the worksheet using P.R.O.
  2. Pairs (7 min): Swap sheets. Partner scores each remix with the Producer's Checklist (on worksheet).
  3. Showcase (5 min): 2–3 students read a "before and after." If devices are available, run the best remix live and critique the output together.

Exit Ticket (5 min)

Bottom of worksheet: students write one P.R.O. prompt they could actually use this week for another class, and name one thing they'd double-check in the AI's answer before trusting it.

Differentiation

  • Support: Provide the P.R.O. sentence starters (Teacher Guide, p.2).
  • Extension: Students write a prompt that produces a BAD answer on purpose, then explain why it fails.
  • No-device classrooms: The teacher plays "the AI" and responds literally to weak prompts — comedy included.
