Teacher Guide

Fact Desk, Frame 1 of the News Desk pack: routine, safe-story rules, script, strong answer, rubric. You play editor, not answer key.

The Routine

45 min: Open 5 — headline only; "What question does this make you want to ask?" · Read 10 — read once, underline claims, pairs retell in one sentence · Frame 20 — solo 12 (steps in order), pair-check 8 (swap, read the evidence, ask one question, fix one thing; "So what?" last) · Share-out 10 — one line per pair, stamp plus evidence; tally RUN / FIX / KILL.

25 min: Open 2 · Read 5 (you read it aloud once; one volunteer retells) · Frame 15 (solo, no pair-check) · Share-out 3 (three volunteers). Collect; the "So what?" lines are the exit ticket.

How to Pick a Safe Story

1. Two sources or it doesn't exist. Two outlets that did not copy each other, or it's a rumor.

2. "Alleged" for any lawsuit. A court hasn't decided. "The musicians allege," never "the company copied."

3. Officeholders as officeholders. "The mayor announced," not the name. Students frame the decision; the person is not the lesson.

4. If students must pick a political side, skip it. A rule, a price, a free app, a hack, a new tool: in. A party, a candidate, a vote: out.

5. Never a story that shames a family. No crime stories with names, no viral videos of kids, nobody a student could be related to.

Fact Desk Script — say it while you circulate

"Underline anything with a name, a date, a number, a place, or a quote in it. If it can be checked, it's a claim." "A second source is not where you first saw it. Same video, same account, same AI: doesn't count." "The stamp is the easy part. Your evidence is the grade."

Strong answer: three or more claims copied exactly, each with a specific kind of second source (a museum site, an encyclopedia entry, the team's own schedule), one stamp, and an evidence line that says whether the story's point still stands.

Full Standards Text

  • ISTE 1.3.b — Students evaluate the accuracy, perspective, credibility and relevance of information, media, data or other resources. Taught: step 3. Assessed: the "Second source" column.
  • CCSS ELA RI.6.8 — Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, distinguishing claims that are supported by reasons and evidence from claims that are not. Taught: steps 1–2. Assessed: verdict + "My evidence."
  • CCSS ELA W.6.1 — Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence. Taught: step 4. Assessed: "My evidence" + "So what?"
