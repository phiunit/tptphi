Teacher Guide

The Routine — 45 minutes

  1. Open 5. Show the headline only. Ask: "What question does this make you want to ask?"
  2. Read 10. Students read once and underline claims. Pairs retell it in one sentence. Partner A goes first: the partner whose first name comes first in the alphabet.
  3. Frame 20. Solo 12: the steps in order. Pair-check 8: swap frames and read the evidence. Partner A asks one question and B answers, then B asks. Swap back, fix one thing, and write the "So what?" last.
  4. Share-out 10. One line per pair: the stamp and the evidence behind it. Tally RUN / FIX / KILL.

Bell-ringer, 25 min: Open 2 · Read 5 (you read it aloud once; one volunteer retells) · Frame 15 (solo, no pair-check) · Share-out 3 (three volunteers). The "So what?" lines are the exit ticket.

How to Pick a Safe Story

1. Two sources or it doesn't exist. Two outlets that didn't copy each other, or it's a rumor. No devices? Read the story aloud, then the second outlet's opening lines. That is the room's second source.

2. "Alleged" for any lawsuit. A court hasn't decided. "The musicians allege," never "the company copied."

3. Officeholders as officeholders. "The mayor announced," not the name. Frame the decision, not the person.

4. If students must pick a political side, skip it. Rules, prices, apps, hacks: in. Parties, candidates, votes: out.

5. Never a story that shames a family. No named crime stories, no viral videos of kids.

Fact Desk Script — say it while you circulate

"Underline anything with a name, a date, a number, a place, or a quote in it. If it can be checked, it's a claim." "A second source is not where you first saw it. Same video, same account, same AI: doesn't count." "The stamp is the easy part. Your evidence is the grade."

Strong answer: three or more claims copied exactly, each with a specific kind of second source (a museum site, an encyclopedia entry, the team's own schedule), one stamp, and an evidence line on whether the story's point still stands.

Full Standards Text

  • ISTE 1.3.b — Students evaluate the accuracy, perspective, credibility and relevance of information, media, data or other resources. Taught: step 3. Assessed: the "Second source" column.
  • CCSS ELA RI.6.8 — Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, distinguishing claims that are supported by reasons and evidence from claims that are not. Taught: steps 1–2. Assessed: verdict + "My evidence."
  • CCSS ELA W.6.1 — Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence. Taught: step 4. Assessed: "My evidence" + "So what?"
