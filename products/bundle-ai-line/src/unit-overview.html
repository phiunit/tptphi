The Through-Line

Human → AI → Human. Every AI job has two human parts: a person starts it (the prompt) and a person ends it (the check). Those two jobs are the whole skill, and they transfer to every AI tool your students will ever touch. Lesson 1 trains the start; Lesson 3 trains the check; Lessons 2, 4, 5 and 6 each show where the human at each end has to step in — the gap in the playlist, the taste file you hand over, the call only a person makes, the loot only the leader checks.

The honesty policy, in one sentence students remember: AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter.

Running It With No Devices

Every lesson is designed to work on paper. In Lessons 1, 2, 4, and 6 the students and teacher play the AI — which is often the better lesson, because a human being deliberately literal is funnier and more memorable than a chatbot. Where devices help, each plan says exactly what they add.

Before You Start

  • Set your class AI rule out loud on day one. "AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter" is the whole policy; Lesson 1's teacher guide has the discussion guide (three questions with answers).
  • You don't need to be an AI expert. Every plan gives you the lines. If a student asks something you can't answer, the honest answer — "let's check that" — is the lesson.
  • Expect the fact-checking lesson to be the sticky one. Students remember catching the machine being wrong.
