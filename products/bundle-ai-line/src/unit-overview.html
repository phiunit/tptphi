The Through-Line

Human → AI → Human. A good AI job has two human parts. A person starts it (the prompt), and a person ends it (the check). Lesson 1 trains both, and Lesson 3 goes deep on the check. Those two jobs carry over to whatever AI tool your students pick up next.

The honesty policy, in one sentence students can remember: AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter.

Before You Start

  • Say your class AI rule out loud on day one. "AI is your editor, not your ghostwriter" is the whole policy. Lesson 1's teacher guide has a three-question talk to go with it.
  • You don't need to be an AI expert. Every plan gives you the lines. If a student asks something you can't answer, say "Let's check that." That honest answer is the lesson.
  • Expect the fact-checking lesson to stick. Students remember the story they almost printed.

Routines That Repeat

Teach these once, and they carry through all six days.

  • Who goes first: the partner whose first name comes first in the alphabet. Lessons 1–3 call that person Partner A, Lessons 4–5 call them Speaker 1, and Lesson 6 uses the same rule to pick who holds the quest card. Same first letter? Check the second letter.
  • Uneven numbers: every plan says how to run one trio (on day 6, a team of 4 or 2).
  • Every lesson ends solo: an exit ticket you collect. Some worksheets call it the Last Check; it's the same routine. The keys are in each teacher guide.
  • The callbacks: Lessons 2, 3, 4, and 6 each reach back to Lesson 1's genie or P.R.O. Each one is an optional aside, so a class that skipped Lesson 1 can skip it too.

Running It With No Devices

Every lesson runs on paper. In Lessons 1, 2, 4, and 6, the students and you play the AI. That is often the better lesson: a classmate playing a twisty genie or a one-playlist app is funnier than a chatbot, and easier to catch. Where devices help, each plan says what they add. Any AI tool a plan mentions runs on your projected screen, never on a student account.

My AI Rules — End of Unit

One page, one question per lesson; it sits next to this overview in the download. Give it the day after Lesson 6, about 15 minutes, solo. Score each row: Got it = the lesson's move, used on something real in the student's life. Getting there = the move is there, but it could fit anyone. Not yet = it only repeats the lesson's words, or the row is blank.
