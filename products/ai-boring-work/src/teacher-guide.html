Why This Lesson

Students hear "AI is taking the jobs" everywhere, usually as a scare. This lesson swaps the fear for a decision-making frame: let the robots do the boring work; leave the humans to do the human things. The honest middle-school version: AI changes jobs more than it deletes them, and every automation choice carries a tradeoff (CSTA 2-IC-20) — that conversation is the intellectual core; the studio is just a stage cool enough to hold it. Student pages say "the trade."

Discussion Moves (for the hard questions)

Q: "Will AI take my mom's job?" — Stay honest, no predictions: "Some parts of almost every job will get automated — usually the repeating parts. Jobs mostly change shape instead of disappearing. The real question: which parts are the human parts? That's your exit ticket."

Q: The class splits three ways on one task — who's right? — Nobody, and say so. Push with: "Convince me. Who wins? Who loses? What breaks?" A three-way split is the best share-out material.

Q: "Isn't automating a task mean to whoever did it?" — Sit in that tension; it IS the standard. Nia, the new helper, who loses the boring texts also loses a way to learn every client's name. Naming both sides out loud is the skill.

Defense Rubric — Defend the Board (SL.6.4)

  • Proficient: one clear claim, a specific detail from the task, names who wins AND who loses or what breaks; audible, looking at the room.
  • Developing: claim plus a reason, but the trade is missing or vague ("it's just faster").
  • Beginning: restates the column with no because, or reads to the desk.

Exit Ticket Exemplar

The job: my uncle's food truck. Automate: posting the truck's daily location to socials. Augment: planning how much food to buy each week — AI predicts, he decides. Keep human: joking with the regulars at the window, because that's why they come back.

Full Standards Text

  • CSTA 2-IC-20 — Compare tradeoffs associated with computing technologies that affect people's everyday activities and career options. Taught: Direct Instruction, the tradeoff lens worked example. Assessed: Trade Cards (worksheet p.2) and Exit Ticket.
  • CCSS ELA SL.6.4 — Present claims and findings, sequencing ideas logically and using pertinent descriptions, facts, and details to accentuate main ideas or themes; use appropriate eye contact, adequate volume, and clear pronunciation. Taught: claim-frame modeling in the Share-Out setup. Assessed: Defend the Board, scored with the Defense Rubric above.
  • ISTE 1.3.d — Students build knowledge by actively exploring real-world issues and problems, developing ideas and theories and pursuing answers and solutions. Taught: The Studio Task Board sort (real tasks, real business). Assessed: Exit Ticket, applying the sort to a real job in their life.
