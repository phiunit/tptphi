A note from Deja, the editor of The Comet, the school paper: "You're on the fact team. Three writers used an AI helper. Remember: AI guesses the most likely words — it does not check them — so it can be completely wrong and still sound sure. No story gets printed until you check it."

Desk words: run = it gets printed  ·  clear = it passes the check  ·  log = write it down  ·  second source = a place to check that is NOT where you first saw the claim  ·  "the ending still stands" = it is still true once the facts are fixed  ·  stamp = your verdict box (RUN, FIX, or KILL) — check one

Your method, every draft:

  • 1. UNDERLINE every checkable claim (names, dates, numbers, places, quotes).
  • 2. RANK — write C above claims to CHECK, S above SAFE ones.
  • 3. LATERAL-READ — leave the draft. Log a second source that could confirm each C.
  • 4. VERDICT — check a stamp, give your evidence, and say if the writer's ending still stands.

Desk Helpers — use these on Draft #3 (and to check #2)

RANK: which claims get a C?

  • Dates, names, numbers, and quotes — easy to get wrong, easy to check.
  • Words like first, only, biggest, tallest — record claims are risky.
  • Anything the ending depends on. If it falls, the story falls.
  • Mark S when everybody already knows it. You can't check everything.

Second sources that count

  • A textbook or an encyclopedia entry on the topic.
  • A museum, space agency, or government website.
  • A different website that did not copy the first one.

Does NOT count: asking the same AI again · the draft itself · "I'd Google it" with no source named.

1. In your own words: how can AI sound 100% sure and still be wrong?
