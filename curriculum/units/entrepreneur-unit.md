# Unit: 21st-Century Entrepreneurship — "Future Builders" (6 lessons, grades 6–8)
Bundle target: bundle-entrepreneur-line ($12–15, not yet in catalog). Every lesson: 45 min,
no-prep, unplugged variant, $4–6 (L1 free). Scope maps to the national CTE Business/Marketing and
Entrepreneurship clusters (state codes vary — cite cluster + CCSS ELA for portability) plus CCSS ELA
argument/presentation and ISTE 1.4 Innovative Designer:
first principles → smallest usable version → who it's for → getting it seen → many attempts →
reach ladder capstone.

## Unit thesis
After six lessons a student can take a vague idea ("someone should fix the lunch line") and walk it
all the way to something a real person has used. They can solve the problem twice — once by copying
and once from the ground up — and say which answer is better and why; strip the idea down to the
smallest version that still works for somebody today; tell the difference between the person who
pays and the person who uses it, and design so both say yes; write a five-step plan for getting it
in front of people and count how many people each step actually reaches; take ten fast attempts and
show what they changed after each one; and finally compute how far the same idea travels when they
deliver it one-on-one versus teaching the people who teach others. The output isn't a business plan
nobody reads — it's a thing that got used, plus a number for how far it could go.

## Unit through-line
**Build it small → put it in somebody's hands → then count the reach.** Every lesson ends with the
same question on the board: *who has actually used this, and how many more could?* L1 and L2 build
small, L3 and L5 put it in hands, L4 and L6 count the reach. The one idea underneath all six is that
having the idea is the cheap half — VOICE stance 21, "you'll be drowned in a sea of unfinished beats
if you never put your work out there." Continuity motif: the same skateboard from L2 rolls across
later pages as a small badge on any step that shipped.

## Lesson 1 — ent-cook-or-chef (FREE lead magnet, $0)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "Cook or Chef?"; FRAMEWORKS "The Cook and the Chef (first principles vs.
  analogy)"; VOICE quote 1 (the cook remixes, the chef invents) and stance 13 (making something out
  of nothing).
- **Why a teacher buys this:** it's a free, zero-prep thinking lesson that fills a full period, works
  as a sub plan, and produces two written ideas from every kid — including the ones who "don't have
  any ideas," because the cook path hands them a starting point.
- **Activity:** teams solve one posted school problem twice — first by copying something that already
  exists and swapping parts (cook), then by listing what the fix must actually *do* and building up
  from that list (chef) — and finish by arguing which of their two answers is better.
- **Assessment:** side-by-side compare sheet plus an exit ticket claim: which answer wins, and the
  two reasons; rubric scores the reasons, not the choice.
- **Unplugged:** entirely paper; no device path needed.

## Lesson 2 — ent-skateboard-first ($4)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "Skateboard, Scooter, Car"; FRAMEWORKS "Skateboards, not cars (Minimum
  Viable Beatmaking)"; VOICE stance 30 (a side-project environment with room to experiment).
- **Why a teacher buys this:** project-based units die in week three because students design
  something they can't finish. This lesson is the fix — it forces a version that's done and usable
  inside one class period, which is what makes every later project day survivable.
- **Activity:** teams take their L1 idea, sketch three rungs (skateboard / scooter / bike), then build
  the skateboard rung out of paper in fifteen minutes and hand it to another team to actually use.
- **Assessment:** skateboard spec sheet plus the other team's honest "did this work for me?" check;
  rubric requires one feature the team deliberately cut and the reason it was safe to cut.
- **Unplugged:** paper, tape, index cards. Device variant is optional only.

## Lesson 3 — ent-who-pays-who-plays ($5)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "Who's the Customer's Customer?"; FRAMEWORKS "Who's the customer's
  customer? (B2B2C)" and "Curse of knowledge" (translate the jargon for the person you're pitching).
- **Why a teacher buys this:** customer-analysis standards usually arrive as a survey worksheet.
  This one turns into role-play in ten minutes and gives the teacher a clean, gradeable artifact —
  and the "who pays vs. who plays" chart transfers straight into any later PBL unit.
- **Activity:** students map one product into two columns — who pays and who plays — list what each
  one needs to hear to say yes, then change one thing about the product so both columns say yes and
  test it by pitching to two classmates playing those two roles.
- **Assessment:** the two-column map, the one change, and a 30-second double pitch scored on whether
  the pitch actually changed between the two listeners.
- **Unplugged:** fully unplugged role-play; no slides.

## Lesson 4 — ent-nobody-came ($5)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "Distribution Is 80%"; FRAMEWORKS "Distribution is 80%"; VOICE stance 20
  ("marketing and distribution is 80% more important").
- **Why a teacher buys this:** every school has the club fair, the fundraiser, the showcase nobody
  came to. This is the lesson that names why, and it converts a marketing standard into a plan the
  teacher can actually reuse for a real school event.
- **Activity:** teams write a five-step plan for getting people to their L2 skateboard version — who
  tells whom, on what day, with what one sentence — then run a paper reach count showing how many
  people each step touches, and swap plans with another team to poke holes.
- **Assessment:** launch plan plus reach-count total plus a "biggest hole in my plan" paragraph the
  student writes about their own plan after the swap; rubric.
- **Unplugged:** paper planning grid; the optional device version drafts the one-sentence hook.
- **Sensitivity note for the teacher guide:** the cold-open scenario is a school club or fundraiser,
  never a birthday party — same lesson, no kid has to relive an empty room of their own.

## Lesson 5 — ent-shots-on-goal ($4)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "Shots on Goal Journal" and "Ten Small Bets Board"; FRAMEWORKS "Shots on
  goal, not hard work" and "Side projects as risk containers"; VOICE stance 18 ("it's actually shots
  on goal pays off").
- **Why a teacher buys this:** it's the growth-mindset day that isn't a poster. Ten timed attempts,
  a log where the adjustment column is what gets graded, and a graph at the end — engagement plus an
  artifact, with nothing to set up but a timer.
- **Activity:** ten rapid rounds of one constrained challenge (pitch it in fifteen seconds, name it
  in three words, draw the logo in sixty seconds), where after every single attempt the student must
  write the one thing they'll change before the next attempt.
- **Assessment:** the ten-row attempt log — score is on the adjustment column and the final graph of
  attempt vs. result, explicitly not on the best attempt; rubric says so out loud to the student.
- **Unplugged:** timer and paper only.

## Lesson 6 — ent-reach-ladder ($6, capstone)
- **Corpus source:** SEEDS "1 to 10,000, The Scaling Ladder"; FRAMEWORKS "The Scaling Ladder" and
  "Leverage = input × multiplier = output"; VOICE stance 22–23 (big impact from little effort; no
  gatekeepers). The "Top 25% + Top 25% = Top 1%" seed rides along as the stretch panel.
- **Why a teacher buys this:** a summative performance task with real math in it. The reach table and
  graph make the abstract word "scale" into two lines on a grid, and the rubric forces students to
  reuse all five earlier artifacts so the teacher grades the unit, not the last day.
- **Activity:** teams compute the reach of their own project four ways — one-on-one, one whole group,
  teaching the people who teach others, and posting it once so it works while they sleep — graph the
  straight line against the multiplying one, pick a rung, and pitch the full plan.
- **Assessment:** reach table, the two-line graph, and a two-minute final pitch; the rubric requires
  the chef-or-cook call from L1, the skateboard from L2, the who-pays/who-plays chart from L3, the
  launch plan from L4, and one adjustment from the L5 log.
- **Unplugged:** hand-drawn graph on the provided grid; live pitch, no slides required.
